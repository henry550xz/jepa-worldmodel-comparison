Working conclusion

What a model encodes, what it predicts, and how a planner reads that prediction are three different questions.

01

The interface can reverse the ranking.

On the original PushT candidate banks, changing only the scorer reverses Gaussian versus Sparse. This does not yet explain the closed-loop success gap.

02

Sparsity is not semantic factorization.

Sparse often responds less to appearance changes, but that does not consistently preserve physical readout accuracy. Causal interventions do not establish uniquely physical sparse coordinates.

03

Spatial access matters for image recovery.

In billiards, Pixel patch features reconstruct held-out images about 50× better than CLS features with equal decoder parameters. This is reconstruction, not future prediction.

We have not established universal JEPA superiority, an optimal depth, general nuisance removal, or a complete causal account of closed-loop failures.

For discussion

The next decision is about reliable interfaces.

The evidence motivates testing spatial prediction and stronger state recovery, while measuring how the two JEPAs respond to scene complexity and appearance. The five-condition ablation keeps the current architecture fixed so its question stays interpretable.
