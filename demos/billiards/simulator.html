WHAT YOU ARE SEEING

One world. Two views.

Colored disks in 2D become spheres in a 3D box. All bodies have equal mass, with no friction or gravity. Collisions are resolved at their event times, not by letting bodies overlap and pushing them apart.

2D follows the research simulator’s collision rules. 3D is an interactive extension with its own physics checks—not a validated 3D learning benchmark. A single camera can hide depth and velocity.

No learned model is running here. Ambiguous simultaneous multi-body impacts pause the demo rather than inventing a collision rule.
